So far, I have lived in more than a dozen cities across five countries on two continents.

Like my upbringing, my career path has had many turns and twists. Before graduate school, I worked as a strategy manager at a software startup. While in college, I served as the youngest member of the user services advisory board at [Naver](https://www.nhn.com/en-US), South Korea’s largest internet company, often called the Korean Google. 

In 2014, I moved to the United States for my doctoral studies with a scholarship from [the Korea Foundation for Advanced Studies](https://eng.kfas.or.kr/theme/kfaschanel/intl_scholarship_5.php).[^kfas] I earned an M.A. and a Ph.D. in political science from UC Berkeley. Before completing my doctoral program, I became a predoctoral fellow at the [SNF Agora Institute at Johns Hopkins University](https://agora.jhu.edu/). There, I co-developed a project that used big data to [map the modern agora](https://snfagora.jhu.edu/our-work/research-projects/mapping-the-modern-agora/) and remained affiliated with the institute for five years. Soon after graduating from the Ph.D. program, I went back to Korea and taught for a year at the [KDI School of Public Policy and Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDI_School_of_Public_Policy_and_Management), South Korea’s premier public policy school.

I then returned to the United States and worked as a data scientist at [Code for America](https://www.codeforamerica.org/), a leading civic tech organization. In that role, I worked with federal, state, and local governments to improve access to safety net programs. After two years in public service, I left Code for America to [return to academia as a policy scholar and public interest data scientist](return2academia.html). I now think of myself as a ["tech and public policy" person](https://jaeyeonkim.substack.com/p/how-i-became-a-tech-and-policy-person).

At the same time, [my perspective and career path have been deeply informed by where I began](memorial_day.html). I’m a first-generation college student from a working-class family. My late father worked as an electrician at paper mills for 32 years. These experiences guide the research questions I pursue and the problems I tackle.
